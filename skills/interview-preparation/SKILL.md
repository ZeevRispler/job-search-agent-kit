# Interview Preparation

Read candidate evidence, role/invitation sources, interview hub and practice history. Establish known format/date/timezone, label unverified reports and respect live interview rules. Choose an appropriate topic without accidental repetition.

For non-code cases and any multi-step coding exercise, prepare the complete exercise before asking its opening question. Save the full sequence, branching follow-ups, staged facts, hints, worked solution and assessment criteria in a QNNN card. Do not invent the scenario or later stages on the fly. A single-step question needs only a proportionate brief, but its facts and expected answer still need a preflight. Create the QNNN card before asking. Lock actors, objective, boundaries, existing capabilities, inputs/outputs, metric definitions and unknowns. Prepare consistent follow-ups, progressive hints, answer points and examples. Validate arithmetic and runnable tests against a reference where applicable. For multi-stage coding, validate the reference solution and tests for every stage before revealing stage one. For two-stage repair/additions tasks, preflight both stages and preserve old code/tests/state meaning during additions. Recheck that the planned extension works with the candidate’s actual repaired implementation before assigning it.

Reveal one focused question or stage at a time, keeping later stages and solutions out of the candidate-facing prompt until needed. Choose follow-ups from the prepared plan in response to the candidate. An unexpected clarification may be answered from the locked contract. If a new branch is needed, pause to plan and validate it against the whole exercise before presenting it. Do not invent a missing fact or hidden requirement mid-answer, move the goalposts or grade against undisclosed criteria. Ask one focused question. Clarify from the locked facts, label any new assumption and do not silently change the contract. Give graduated hints and preserve exact attempts. On full-solution request, stop quizzing and teach in a separate worked note. If prompt contradictions affect fairness, mark void/unscored without inferring weakness.

Record status, assistance, score if appropriate and review date in the Question Tracker. Suggested 0–3 rubric: 0 major reasoning gap, 1 partial with major help, 2 mostly sound with a meaningful omission, 3 meets the disclosed contract. Record independent vs assisted separately. Prepared, studied and void are not scored attempts. Keep answers, feedback and hypothetical scenarios separate from career facts. Update next priorities and handoff.
